# Project governance

PX4 Dataset Builder is maintained as an independent Apache-2.0 community project. Its technical direction is governed by public evidence, project scope, maintainability, privacy, and interoperability rather than by access to private technology or data.

## Decision process

- Small, reversible fixes are decided through pull-request review.
- Public schema, methodology, dependency, compatibility, or privacy changes begin with an issue or Discussion.
- Decisions should record the problem, alternatives, evidence, trade-offs, and migration impact.
- Compatibility claims require reproducible fixtures or measurements; votes and commercial interest are not evidence.
- When consensus is unavailable, maintainers choose the smallest reversible option consistent with published scope and explain the decision publicly.

## Roles

- **Contributors** propose and review code, documentation, methodology, or compatibility evidence.
- **Reviewers** provide sustained technical review but do not necessarily hold repository permissions.
- **Maintainers** merge changes, manage releases and security reports, moderate community spaces, and enforce privacy and licensing boundaries.

Maintainer access is based on sustained public contribution and review. It is not granted in exchange for funding, employment, data access, or commercial partnership.

## Releases and compatibility

Milestones are exit criteria, not promised dates. Maintainers may delay a release when evidence, migration documentation, privacy review, or reproducibility is incomplete. Deprecations and schema changes must be documented before removal and follow semantic versioning once the relevant interface is declared stable.

## Data decisions

Maintainers may reject or remove a fixture when ownership, consent, redistribution rights, privacy, safety, or provenance are unclear. Dataset acceptance is separate from software contribution acceptance. A public issue or private transfer does not create an obligation to retain or publish data.

## Conflicts of interest

Contributors and maintainers should disclose a material employer, sponsor, dataset-owner, or vendor interest when it could influence a technical or data-governance decision. Disclosure does not disqualify participation; it allows reviewers to evaluate the context.

No organization is entitled to preferential roadmap, compatibility, or public claims. If the maintainer group becomes diverse and sustained, transfer to a neutral community organization may be considered through a public governance proposal.
